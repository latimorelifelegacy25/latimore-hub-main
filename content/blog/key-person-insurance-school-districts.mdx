## What Is Key Person Insurance?

Key person insurance is a life insurance policy purchased by an organization on the life of an individual whose death or permanent disability would cause significant financial or operational harm to that organization. The organization owns the policy, pays the premiums, and receives the death benefit. In a corporate context, it's been standard practice for decades. In educational institutions, it remains dramatically underutilized — not because it's less important, but because it's less discussed.

## How a Policy Is Structured

- **Owner:** The school district
- **Insured:** The designated key administrator
- **Beneficiary:** The school district
- **Death benefit:** Paid to the district upon the insured's death

The insured administrator must consent to and cooperate with the application process, including underwriting. The policy does not require the administrator's participation in day-to-day management — it is a financial instrument held by the district, not the individual.

## How the Benefit Amount Is Determined

A reasonable starting point is the estimated cost of a two-year transition. That estimate typically includes emergency administrative search fees ($25,000–$60,000+ for superintendent searches), interim administrator costs, consulting and knowledge reconstruction, and a reserve buffer. For most small to mid-sized Pennsylvania districts, a policy of $250,000–$750,000 per key position provides meaningful protection.

## What It Costs

For a district insuring a 50-year-old superintendent with $500,000 in coverage, annual premiums are typically in the range of $3,000–$8,000. Against a district operating budget of several million dollars, that's a small line item for meaningful protection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the administrator have to agree?** Yes. The insured individual must provide written consent and cooperate with underwriting.

**What if the administrator leaves before they die?** The policy lapses or can be surrendered. Some policies have cash value the district receives on surrender.

**Can the administrator take the policy with them?** No. The district owns the policy. It cannot be transferred to the individual without the district's consent.

**Is this taxable to the district?** Death benefits received are generally excluded from federal income tax when applicable requirements are met. Districts should consult their solicitor for guidance specific to their situation.

## Why Districts Overlook It

Most boards plan for the things they can see: buses, roofs, contracts, pensions. The sudden loss of a superintendent, a business manager, or a transportation director doesn't show up on a capital plan, so it never gets a line in the budget.

But I've sat with enough district leaders to know what happens next. The work doesn't stop. Someone has to sign the payroll, answer the state, and hold the community together, and that someone is usually a person already carrying a full load. Interim leadership, a search, and months of lost momentum cost real money, and the budget has no cushion for any of it.

## What a Sudden Vacancy Actually Looks Like

Think about the first 90 days after an unexpected loss:

- An interim leader has to be found, contracted, and brought up to speed.
- A search firm may be needed, and searches take months, not weeks.
- Projects, grant deadlines, and negotiations slow down while the board decides what comes first.
- Other administrators absorb extra work, and that has its own cost.

None of this is a criticism of anyone's planning. It is simply the part of the picture most districts have never priced out. Key person coverage doesn't replace a person. It gives the district liquid funds to manage the transition without pulling from classroom or operating dollars.

## Questions Every Board Should Ask

Before approving anything, I'd want your board to be able to answer these:

1. **Which positions are truly critical?** Not every title is. Start with the one or two roles where a vacancy would do the most damage.
2. **What would a two-year transition cost us?** Use your own numbers. The ranges in this article are estimates, and your district's costs will differ.
3. **Do we have the legal authority?** Public-sector rules vary. Have your solicitor confirm what board action, notice, and written consent are required.
4. **Who owns the policy and who receives the benefit?** For a key person arrangement, the district should be both owner and beneficiary.
5. **What happens if the administrator leaves?** Know the surrender, lapse, and transfer terms before you sign.

## Compliance Points to Confirm First

Employer-owned life insurance carries specific notice and consent requirements, and carriers require an employer-owned policy form for key person arrangements. The insured must give written consent, and underwriting approval is not guaranteed. Premium amounts depend on age, health, and coverage amount, and the ranges above are only illustrations. Tax treatment depends on your district's circumstances, so please review it with your solicitor or tax advisor.
