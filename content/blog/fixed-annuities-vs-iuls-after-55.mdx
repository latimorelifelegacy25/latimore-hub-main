## Two Products. Both Legitimate. Only One Is Right for Your Situation.

Both fixed indexed annuities (FIAs) and indexed universal life insurance (IUL) can credit interest based on a market index. Neither directly invests your money in the index, and downside protections depend on contract terms, policy costs, riders, and carrier guarantees. Both are tools used by serious retirement planners. They are not the same product, and they don't serve the same purpose.

## Fixed Indexed Annuities (FIA)

A fixed indexed annuity is a contract with an insurance company. You deposit a lump sum, and the carrier protects the contract value from direct market-index loss, subject to contract terms, fees, riders, and surrender charges. Interest is credited based on the performance of a market index (usually the S&P 500), subject to a cap, spread, or participation rate.

- **Core purpose:** Contract-Based Income
- **Best for:** Retirees or near-retirees who need contract-based income to cover fixed expenses with principal protection
- **Tax treatment:** Grows tax-deferred; income is taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn
- **Carriers we work with:** American Equity, F&G, Corebridge Financial

## Indexed Universal Life (IUL)

An IUL is a permanent life insurance policy with a cash value component linked to a market index. Unlike a FIA, an IUL's primary purpose is the death benefit. The cash value can be accessed through policy loans, whose tax treatment depends on policy design, MEC status, and keeping the policy in force. Loans accrue interest and reduce policy values; lapse or surrender can create taxable income.

- **Core purpose:** Death benefit + tax-advantaged cash value accumulation
- **Best for:** People who want permanent insurance AND a tax-advantaged accumulation vehicle; supplemental retirement income in higher tax brackets
- **Risk:** Must be properly funded and monitored; underfunded IULs can collapse

## The Direct Answer

If lifetime income is a priority, review whether an annuity and any optional income rider fit your expenses, liquidity needs, time horizon, and other income sources. Fees, surrender terms, and insurer claims-paying ability matter.

If your primary question is "How do I supplement retirement income in a tax-efficient way while also leaving a death benefit?" — the answer leans toward **IUL**, assuming you have the time horizon and premium capacity to fund it properly.

Many pre-retirees benefit from both: a FIA to cover essential income needs, and an IUL started earlier to build tax-advantaged supplemental income over time.

## What Each One Costs You

Neither product is free, and I'd be doing you a disservice if I only talked about the upside.

**Fixed indexed annuity:**

- Surrender charges and a market value adjustment may apply if you take money out early.
- Withdrawals may be taxable, and taking money before age 59½ may carry an additional tax penalty.
- Caps, spreads, and participation rates can change at the insurer's discretion, so the rate you see today may not be the rate you see in year five.
- Optional income riders may have a fee, a waiting period, or rules about when income can begin.

**Indexed universal life:**

- Policy charges, including the cost of insurance, come out of the policy every month.
- Loans accrue interest and reduce both the cash value and the death benefit.
- If the policy lapses or is surrendered with an outstanding loan, the result can be taxable income.
- A policy that is overfunded beyond federal limits can become a modified endowment contract (MEC), which changes the tax treatment of loans and withdrawals.
- Your health matters. IUL requires underwriting, and what you pay depends on your age and health.

## Three Questions That Sort It Out

When someone sits across from me at 57 or 60, these are the questions that usually point the way:

1. **Do you need income you can count on to cover fixed bills, or are you looking for extra income on top of what you already have?** The first points toward looking hard at an annuity. The second opens the door to looking at IUL.
2. **Do you want to leave a death benefit?** If yes, an IUL is built for that. An annuity can leave a benefit to heirs, but that isn't its main job.
3. **How long can you leave the money alone?** Both products reward patience and penalize early exits. If you might need the money in three years, neither is the right fit.

## Who Should Probably Wait

Some people should slow down before buying either one:

- If you have high-interest debt or no emergency savings, fix that first.
- If you may need this money within the next few years, a contract with surrender terms is the wrong place for it.
- If your health is a concern, ask about underwriting before you build a plan around an IUL.
- If you haven't maxed out the tax-advantaged accounts you already have, ask your tax professional where this money fits.

## One Honest Caveat

Everything I've described depends on the specific contract, the specific carrier, and your specific situation. Guarantees in either product are backed by the claims-paying ability of the issuing insurance company, and illustrated values are not guaranteed. Please consult your tax professional or attorney about how these products fit your plan. A conversation is a good place to start, and it won't cost you anything.
